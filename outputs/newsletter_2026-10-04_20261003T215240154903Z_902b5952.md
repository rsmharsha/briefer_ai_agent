# Climate and Clean Energy Brief

**Date:** 2026-10-04

Here’s a simple roundup of recent climate and renewable energy news. Main themes: a strengthening El Niño, NOAA’s October outlook for warmer and mixed rainfall patterns in the U.S., and continued clean power growth even as obstacles increase.

## Climate Change Updates

NOAA says El Niño is strengthening, with a greater than 90% chance of a very strong event during fall and winter 2026–27. It also says there is a 75% chance that October–December 2026 could reach historic strength compared with El Niño events since 1950. Pacific ocean temperatures are far above average in the eastern equatorial Pacific, and atmospheric patterns also match a stronger El Niño. NOAA adds an important caution: El Niño raises the odds of certain impacts, but does not guarantee them.

For October 2026, NOAA favors warmer-than-normal conditions across much of the western U.S., plus parts of the Plains, Upper Mississippi Valley, Gulf Coast, and Florida. It also favors wetter-than-normal conditions across much of the southern U.S., especially near the Gulf Coast and Texas Hill Country, while much of the West is favored to be drier than normal. Some areas remain in NOAA’s “equal chances” category, meaning no strong tilt either way.

## Renewable Energy News

An EDF and Atlas Public Policy report says U.S. clean power kept growing in the first half of 2026. The country added 26 gigawatts of clean power, including 11.5 GW of solar, 8.3 GW of battery storage, and 6.3 GW of wind. Total operating clean power capacity reached 489.6 GW. Looking ahead, solar and batteries make up about 87% of planned and under-construction clean capacity.

The report also says the buildout is facing stronger headwinds. Developers canceled 14.9 GW of clean projects, while planned and under-construction natural gas capacity rose by 23.5 GW to 68.3 GW. The summary points to permitting delays, changes to federal tax credits, and more gas buildout as obstacles even while clean power keeps expanding.

## What It Means in Simple Terms

These updates show two sides of the story. On the climate side, a stronger El Niño can shift the odds toward certain weather patterns, and NOAA’s October outlook points to warmer conditions in many western areas and wetter conditions across much of the southern U.S. But these are outlooks, not certainties.

On the energy side, clean power is still expanding, especially solar and batteries, but progress is uneven. One practical example is DOE’s ETIPP program, which is accepting applications from coastal, remote, and island communities for technical help on issues like energy storage, grid systems, workforce development, and planning. Selected communities can receive up to 24 months of assistance, and some may also qualify for direct funding. It’s a reminder that energy progress depends on planning and local support, not just technology.
